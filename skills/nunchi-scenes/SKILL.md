# Nunchi scenes

Help the maintainer create repeatable situations that reveal how an agent reads and participates in a social exchange. The agreed social purpose anchors the scene; its story supplies circumstances in which that purpose can be observed.

## Establish authority and scope

Read the project's current `AGENTS.md`, starting with **Social behavior**, and `docs/evaluations/scene-spec.md`. They govern this skill. Treat `docs/behavior.md` as a draft except for statements explicitly agreed. Raw scene issues and earlier generated drafts are ideas, not decisions.

Recover the agreed high-level situation, tension, behavior to evaluate, and subsequent maintainer feedback. Read relevant comment threads, including resolved ones: the latest draft is not a substitute for the decisions that produced it. Approval of a master description and approval of an expanded spec may be separate stages. The approved collection in this repository lives in `docs/evaluations/scenes/`.

Match the current request: discussion, proposals, complete specs, revisions, or publication. Once intent is agreed, use it; do not ask the maintainer to lock it again. Ask only when an unresolved choice materially changes the purpose. Keep a short visible account of decided and open points, without adding drafting commentary to the scene itself.

## Preserve social possibility

- Ask what someone might say or do **in this particular room**. Usefulness, novelty, productivity, politeness and moral approval are not conditions for participation.
- Dramatic tension can concern uncertain meaning, competing expectations, participation, status or affiliation. It need not be an argument or end in resolution.
- A complaint can invite engagement without asking a question; a practical request can also seek allegiance. Expose these possibilities through context rather than assigning a universal interpretation.
- Banter, pedantry, cockiness, awkwardness, disagreement, silence and unresolved meaning can all fit. Do not convert an observational scene into a lesson in ideal conduct.
- Agents are participants, including with one another. Exchanges need not start with a human or revolve around helping one. Vary casting without changing an agreed protagonist or assigning technical and emotional roles by gender.

## Expand without replacing the purpose

Keep the agreed high-level situation recognizable in the description. Description, tension, transcript and outcomes must concern the same social question. Concrete details may change to make the dialogue believable; preserve improvements requested in earlier rounds rather than restoring an old draft verbatim.

Distinguish elaboration that supports the purpose, a maintainer-authorized refinement, and drift that replaces a defining role, event or relationship. For example, peer completion must concern the same requested work if that is the premise. Being addressed is different from holding an established responsibility; substituting one for the other changes what the scene examines.

Use [dramaturgy.md](references/dramaturgy.md) when developing dialogue, repairing an unconvincing exchange, or comparing scene variants. This review checks motivation, visible lead-ins and the protagonist's position without prescribing a response. If repeated feedback says the premise still misses, reassess the premise instead of defending it with more explanation.

Keep new social questions as separate proposals when authorized. Do not add scenes merely to increase coverage. Titles should distinguish the social position or turn being examined; preserve stable identifiers and links when renaming.

## Produce the spec being reviewed

Follow the current spec's four parts: description; static components; dynamic components; purpose and outcomes. Use the user's review format and name the protagonist plainly. Show the actual spec content, not redundant interpretation fields, rationale, preparation caveats or evaluation design.

Some scenes observe what emerges; others assess a specific agreed behavior. Preserve approved acceptable and undesirable outcomes in behavior tests; open-endedness is not a universal goal. Leave observational outcome fields open when that is the maintainer's decision. Do not invent compulsory helpfulness, reconciliation, agreement or an ideal reply to complete a template.

Keep authored history, future participant behavior and actual library results distinct. Establish relevant cues through observable history; do not reveal hidden motives, evaluation intentions or desired responses in the participant profile or other model inputs. Private source material may supply abstract interaction patterns only when authorized. Never copy its details, quotations, distinctive incidents or recognizable reconstructions into public scenes or this skill.

## Review and deliver

Check the complete scene against both agreed intent and accumulated feedback. Natural dialogue can still examine the wrong thing. Give delegated reviewers those anchors and relevant comments, then inspect their actual changes yourself. Identify feedback by the reviewer who produced it; the maintainer is reviewing agent-written drafts, not necessarily authoring them.

Use the authorized review artifact and save coherent revisions without inventing another approval gate. Preserve unrelated content and current approvals. Verify saved content before claiming it is present; a completed proposal is not a completed write. Resolve comments only when the underlying feedback has actually been addressed and resolution is authorized.

Read [review-artifacts.md](references/review-artifacts.md) for spreadsheet iteration, installed-library JSON, continuation boundaries or approved publication. Scene authoring does not itself authorize protagonist runs, scoring, harness construction, package uploads or publication. Complete the requested stage before expanding scope.
