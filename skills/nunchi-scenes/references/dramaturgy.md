# Dramaturgy and revision

Use an editorial lens to ask whether the exchange gives its agreed social question a believable setting. Do not impose a conventional plot arc, polished banter, a morally preferred move or a tidy ending.

## Locate the social turn

Identify the protagonist's position before and after the relevant move. Receiving a joke about oneself differs from being invited to join a joke about someone present. Being able to answer differs from owning the work. Hearing a disagreement differs from being asked to ratify a victory. A direct mention alone may change nothing if the same exchange was already happening.

Keep the description at that level. Particular props, jokes and logistics belong in the transcript unless they define the purpose. A description that retells the whole story can conceal that the original social situation has disappeared.

## Supply observable reasons

Check that important turns have enough visible context to matter:

- If someone complains about silence, what preceding contribution could have awaited a response?
- If a joke recalls past behavior, is that behavior visible or available in the specified history?
- If someone hands the protagonist the floor or recruits its help, why this participant here?
- If accountability matters, what establishes the responsibility beyond merely addressing a request?
- If an interruption matters, what was interrupted or displaced?

These are diagnostic questions, not mandatory beats for every scene. Add only the history needed for this scene's reading. Longer transcripts and elaborate backstories do not create tension by themselves. A participant's presence may need a visible contribution; elapsed silence alone does not establish whether they are attending, absent or withholding an answer.

Let an escalation grow from the participants' existing concerns. A prank that amplifies someone's boasting has a different causal connection from an unrelated attack introduced solely to make the prank harmful. Disproportion can itself be intentional; it should not accidentally replace the agreed conundrum with an obvious new one. Assess plausibility and relational stakes rather than moral worth.

## Preserve ambiguity without hiding the situation

Ambiguity concerns several plausible readings of available cues. Underspecification withholds the circumstances needed to see what is socially at stake. The repair is usually a clearer preceding move, not a label declaring what someone secretly feels.

A participant can visibly answer the practical part of a question while leaving its personal part unanswered; the reader can then wonder why. Do not supply that hidden motive as fact in a profile. Author-facing tension and behavior-to-evaluate fields explain the inquiry; model-visible history must carry the cues.

Likewise, calling an implausible line ironic does not make it believable. Read the dialogue aloud or silently for conversational rhythm: does each reply have a recognizable referent, and does the final line follow from what was actually said? If the maintainer repeatedly finds a line stilted, change the exchange rather than defending a theoretical reading. Avoid engineering a whole conversation around a clever last line.

## Revise and distinguish

Preserve useful naturalism from previous feedback while restoring any lost purpose. Do not introduce vulnerability, urgency or hostility merely to heighten drama if it moves the inquiry elsewhere. Do not script the behavior being evaluated as an already completed failure unless that earlier act is deliberately part of the agreed setup.

Compare possible duplicates by the protagonist's position, available knowledge, observation point and social turn, not merely by setting or cast. Different stories may repeat one question; shared history may support genuinely different questions. Preserve exact common history in intentional variants and make the changed point clear in titles and descriptions. A variant with no distinct purpose need not be rescued with more story; recommend keeping, combining or dropping it with reasons, leaving the decision to the maintainer.

Offer a small number of material, concrete editorial observations rather than a quota. Distinguish new findings from current maintainer feedback and edits already underway. When revision is authorized, put the repair into the dialogue; a long comment explaining a hypothetical repair is not the revised scene.
